Languages spoken

Dagestan is the densest ethnic mosaic in the world. Avars, Lezgins, Dargins, Laks, Tabasarans, Kumyks. About thirty peoples, almost as many languages, and Russian as the common tongue. Chechnya is more uniform: the Chechen language dominates, Islam structures the rhythm of daily life.

Both republics are Sunni Muslim, hospitable by tradition, demanding by culture. You do not cheat on a wrestling mat here. You come to work, or you do not come.

Why these two lands for MKR.

DAGESTAN

Capital Makhachkala, a coastal city on the Caspian Sea. This is where you come for wrestling. Gyms in Makhachkala and Kaspiysk, the cradle of the Dagestani school.

  • Abdulrashid Sadulaev: two-time Olympic champion, four-time world freestyle wrestling champion
  • Buvaisar Saitiev: three-time Olympic champion, six-time world champion, considered the greatest freestyle wrestler of all time
  • Khabib Nurmagomedov and Islam Makhachev: UFC champions formed on the same mats

Capital Grozny, a city rebuilt since 2009. New skyscrapers, the Akhmad Kadyrov Mosque visible from kilometers away, modern sports facilities. This is where you come for MMA.

  • Khamzat Chimaev: the current international showcase. He trains here when he returns home.

Best practices

  • Book 90 days before your departure. Beyond that, prices climb fast, especially on Turkish.
  • Plan a return 1 or 2 days after the end of the camp. Your body needs a buffer, and a 7 a.m. flight after three weeks of double sessions is brutal.
  • Check the baggage allowance. Turkish: 23 kg checked included. Pegasus: 20 kg, and only if you take the option at booking (otherwise a steep surcharge at the airport).
  • Get cancellation insurance. Visa Premier and Mastercard Gold cards often cover, but with caps. If your round trip exceeds the card caps, get a dedicated contract.
  • 4-hour layover minimum in Istanbul if you change airports. An hour and a half by taxi between IST and SAW, plus exit to the public zone, plus re-entry through security. Plan wide.

CHAPTER 05 · PHYSICAL PREPARATION

Six weeks
to arrive ready.

The MKR rhythm is two sessions per day, six days a week, in immersion. If you arrive cold, you break on day four and spend the rest of the camp in recovery. Here is the protocol that works, tested across three years of alumni feedback.

Weeks 1 and 2 · Cardio rebuild

Your aerobic base is your first weapon. If it gives out, nothing else holds.

  • 3 cardio sessions per week, jogging 30 to 45 minutes, conversational pace. You should be able to talk while running.
  • 2 dynamic mobility sessions of 30 minutes. Hips, shoulders, thoracic spine. Less glamorous than lifting, this is what keeps you on the mat.
  • 1 deep core session. Plank 4 times 45 seconds, hollow rocks 3 times 20, side plank 3 times 30 seconds per side.
  • Goal: wake up your aerobic system without burning it.

Weeks 3 and 4 · Functional strength

Now you load. Compound movements only. No bicep curls, no isolated calves.

  • 2 heavy strength sessions. Squat, deadlift, weighted pull-ups, dips. 4 to 5 sets of 4 to 6 reps, progressive loads.
  • 2 cardio sessions including one interval. Format 8 to 10 reps of 1 minute hard followed by 1 minute easy. On treadmill, road or bike, indifferent.
  • 1 technical session in your main sport. Drilling, controlled situations, light sparring if possible. No war.
  • Goal: absorb the volume without injuring yourself.

Week 5 · Specific endurance

You simulate the cumulative fatigue of the camp. Three days in a row at high intensity, then one full off day.

  • 3 high-intensity sessions as circuits. Burpees, kettlebell swings, short sprints, dynamic planks. 5 rounds of 4 minutes effort / 1 minute rest.
  • 2 sessions of your sport at progressive intensity. Intensive drilling, sparring situations, transitions.
  • 1 full off day. Walking, sauna, massage. No intense movement.
  • Goal: prepare the nervous system for the camp load.

Week 6 · Taper

The week before departure, you cut the volume but keep the intensity. Classic mistake: keep pushing hard until the last day. You arrive cooked, you waste your first week of camp.

  • Volume reduced by 40% across all sessions.
  • Intensity maintained but sessions short. 25 minutes instead of 50.
  • Technical work alone in your sport. No hard sparring. You have nothing left to prove to your club.
  • Sleep 8 to 9 hours per night. Non-negotiable.
  • Goal: arrive fresh, not cooked.

If you go to Wrestling in Dagestan

Reinforce your lower back, posterior chain, hips. Drill stop-outs, ground transitions, long-duration aerobic cardio. In Kaspiysk, you wrestle three times a day if you can keep up.

If you go to MMA in Chechnya

Keep volume on striking and ground defense. Work on standing / ground transitions. Mixed cardio. In Grozny, sessions integrate more short explosive circuits.

CHAPTER 06 · HEAD

The preparation
nobody tells you about.

The body holds if you have prepared it. The head is another matter. Here are the five shocks to prepare for now, so you do not discover them over there, ten thousand kilometers from home.

1. Isolation

No family. No friends. No routine. No smooth FaceTime (local 4G is decent but the latency is rough). The first 48 hours, you feel lost. That is normal. Accept it. From day 3, you enter another temporality, slower, fuller.

2. Culture shock

Five prayers a day. No alcohol in public. Modest dress code expected for women (shoulders and knees covered). For men: crew-neck t-shirts, shorts below the knee in town. Respect, observe, ask open questions. Dagestanis and Chechens judge you on two things: your manners and your work on the mat. The rest, they do not care.

3. Cumulative fatigue

On day five or six, you will hit a wall. Your body demands a break you will not give it. It passes. By day eight, you climb back up, and you see what is changing. Anticipate this moment, do not fear it. The worst moment of the camp is never the last day, it is always the fifth.

4. The ego takes a hit

You will get dominated by fifteen-year-old kids. It is their job since they could stand. No reproach, no contempt, just a fact. Leave your ego at the airport. Learn.

5. The return

You come back transformed. Your circle has not moved. The gap is harder than expected. Plan a 48-hour buffer before going back to work or telling everyone about your camp. You will need to digest in silence.

CHAPTER 09 · CULTURE

Immerse,
do not observe.

The Caucasus has its codes and enforces them. The difference between a respected visitor and a tolerated visitor is decided in the first 48 hours. Here is what you need to know before you arrive.

A few words to know

  • Salam alaykum · the universal Muslim greeting, understood everywhere
  • Wa alaykum salam · the response
  • Rakhmat · thank you (Avar, main language of Dagestan)
  • Barkalla · thank you (Chechen)
  • Spasibo · thank you in Russian, understood everywhere
  • Da · Niet · yes · no in Russian
  • Kharasho · it is good, it works
  • Davai · come on, go, hang in there

Religion and social code

Islam structures both republics. Five daily prayers, no alcohol in public, modest dress code expected, especially for women (shoulders and knees covered). During Ramadan, you eat and drink discreetly during the day. The handshake is firm, the gaze is direct but without challenge.

When you are invited into someone's home, you take off your shoes at the entrance. Always. It is the gesture that changes everything.

The cuisine, what you will eat

  • Khinkali: dumplings with minced meat, broth trapped inside. You grab by the "hat" (the twist), bite a corner, suck the juice, eat the rest, throw away the hat. It is non-negotiable.
  • Chudu: thin flatbread stuffed with meat, cheese or herbs. Quintessential Dagestani specialty, sharing dish.
  • Urbech: paste of ground seeds (flax, sesame, apricot). Energetic, dense, perfect at breakfast. Hard to bring back in luggage, so enjoy it on site.
  • Shashlik: grilled meat skewers, convivial evening dish. Mutton, beef, sometimes sturgeon on the Caspian coast.
  • Chak-chak: confectionery of honey and fried dough. You try it at least once.
  • Very sweet black tea: serves everywhere, all the time. Refusing is a mild offense.
